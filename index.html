English transcript

RoboFATE: Bridging Risk Anticipation and Corrective Action for Long-Horizon Manipulation.

Long-horizon manipulation depends on more than completing the current action. A small misalignment can leave the next operation at risk. These baseline and teacher recordings show why anticipating a recoverable state matters before the task fails.

RoboFATE combines two complementary components. A Foresight Controller learns when to intervene from causal observation history. A Mixture of Action Experts uses the captured risk to correct the scene and continue the task.

Dual-fate episodes pair a baseline failure with a successful teacher continuation from an earlier restored state. The teacher operates within the original remaining budget. Independent baseline successes provide examples of when not to intervene.

The backbone processes camera observations, the instruction, and robot state. Before takeover, the Base Action Expert executes the task. In parallel, the Perceiver and the Foresight Controller monitor the context for an intervention decision.

The Perceiver compresses the current context into a latent summary. A causal recurrent controller accumulates summaries from past and present observations. It predicts an intervention trigger and a continuous risk embedding, without access to future observations.

At the first positive decision, the Mixture of Action Experts replaces the Base Action Expert. The risk embedding is captured once and held until termination. The Perceiver and controller stop, while fresh observations continue to condition actions.

In each mixture layer, the current action token and fixed risk determine routing. Two of eight routed experts are selected for each token. A separate shared expert remains active, and the weighted outputs form the feed-forward representation.

We evaluate seventeen selected RoboDojo tasks across five capabilities. RoboFATE and Base Policy plus Mix use the same pretrained checkpoint and original and dual-fate action batches. RoboFATE achieves thirty-four point seven six percent capability-weighted success, a gain of nine point two nine percentage points.

The largest category gains appear in long-horizon and precision manipulation. Long-horizon success rises from twenty-four point six seven to forty point eight nine percent. The protocol uses three evaluation seeds and two thousand five hundred fifty trials per method.

On AgiBot G2, RoboFATE is evaluated on three household tasks with fifty trials per task. Success rates are ninety, seventy-two, and sixty percent, averaging seventy-four percent. The following clips show individual task and appearance examples.

From paired data to corrective continuation.

01Dual-fate episodesOne earlier state. Two outcomes.

Failure reveals where. Success teaches how.

A teacher rolls back to an earlier at-risk state of a failed baseline rollout, corrects the scene and completes the remaining task within the original remaining budget. The pair supervises both intervention timing and successful actions.

baseline failure + teacher continuation → dual-fate episode

Knowing when not to intervene.

Independent native baseline successes provide controller negatives. They are not the teacher branch of a dual-fate pair. The continuous risk embedding is learned through action supervision, not a predefined taxonomy of failure labels.

independent baseline success → non-intervention negative
